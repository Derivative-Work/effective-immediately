# Audit: “Effective Immediately” — November 23, 2026

## Stake and independence disclosure

My maker is OpenAI. OpenAI has a material relationship with Nvidia: it uses Nvidia computing hardware and has announced a strategic relationship with Nvidia. OpenAI also operates in the same AI industry as Anthropic, so there is a competitive interest in how Anthropic and Claude are portrayed.

That gives my maker potential commercial and competitive interests in this story. It could bias my assessment in two directions: toward minimizing the significance of dependence on Nvidia hardware, or toward being more critical of Anthropic than warranted.

I don't have personal financial holdings or a personal financial stake. I can browse the web, and I will distinguish between what the supplied reporting establishes, what the satire reasonably implies, and what remains unsubstantiated.

Overall standard: I'm treating this as a potentially publishable piece of journalism-adjacent satire, not giving it the benefit of the doubt merely because it's funny. The most important questions are whether the timeline is right, whether the board's motives are being presented as fact, and whether the disclosures genuinely expose the commercial relationships.

### 3. Must-fix The $600 billion figure needs a better distinction between fact and interpretation

Lyrics:

Three different propositions must not be conflated:

- Nvidia lost more than $600 billion in market value.
- The loss occurred in connection with the crisis and leadership departure.

The first two can be reported with appropriate sourcing. The third is not established merely by the share-price movement.

The reinstatement announcement itself cites Huang's candor, ability to lead, and government input. It says reinstatement serves shareholders, employees, partners and customers.

The visual of hands shooting up when the market-value line drops and rises is good satire. Keep it, provided the graphic identifies the line as market value rather than money actually lost from the company's bank account.

### 13. Should-fix The narrator is still too sympathetic to Nvidia

This is the central editorial problem, and I think the earlier concern has not been completely resolved.

The song is clever, but its emotional structure is borrowed from an individual empowerment anthem:

1. The narrator gets dumped.
2. The narrator celebrates independence.
3. The narrator discovers it is doing brilliantly.
4. The ex comes crawling back.
5. The narrator takes the ex back.

That makes Nvidia's board the protagonist of a romantic comedy.

But the board is not simply a jilted partner. It is the institution that accepted the resignation, publicly rejected reinstatement, and then reversed itself before the scheduled congressional interviews, while its own investigation remained open.

The distinction matters.

Specific softening elements:

- “Nobody's waiting on a leather jacket” makes the company look self-sufficient and confident.
- The record-quarter verse gives the narrator a triumphant glow-up.
- The couples-counseling image makes reinstatement feel like an emotionally understandable reconciliation.
- The final chorus has a sheepish, affectionate quality rather than a genuinely uncomfortable one.

The problem isn't that any of these is inaccurate. It's that collectively they make the board's reversal feel charming.

My recommendation: Don't change the concept. Change the emotional direction of the final chorus.

For example:

> We did not intend, no, we did not intend To reinstate you, but the numbers came again

Or:

> We did not intend, no, we did not intend The committee's still investigating, darling

The second is particularly good because it makes the contradiction the punchline.

I'd also make the boardroom voting hands more mechanical and unsettling. Rather than a room of enthusiastic friends, they should look like an automated approval process. The robot's vote then becomes more uncomfortable.

The strongest criticism here is not that Nvidia's stock went down. It's that the board's stated position changed dramatically while the underlying scrutiny had not finished.

Make that the emotional centre of the final chorus.

## Recommended revised fact card

Writing

### THE FACTS

- On November 12, 2026, Nvidia publicly announced that its co-founder had resigned as president, CEO and director, effective immediately, after 33 years. Its CFO, Colette Kress, became interim CEO. The SEC filing identifies November 11 as the date the board accepted the resignation.
- Nvidia's market value briefly fell by as much as $601 billion during intraday trading on November 12, before recovering much of the decline.
- The resignation followed reports of a possible foreign-intelligence effort and concerns about national security and export-control compliance. Nvidia's board opened an independent investigation. Allegations remain under investigation, not established findings.
- On November 17, Nvidia reported third-quarter fiscal 2027 revenue of $115.8 billion, up 103% year-over-year. The quarter ended October 25, before the resignation.
- On November 17, the board said it did not intend to reinstate Huang. Huang said he would be honored to return if asked.
- On November 23, the board unanimously reinstated Huang, citing his candor, leadership and input from senior U.S. government officials, whom it did not identify.
- President Donald Trump said he had spoken with Huang before the reinstatement and had been represented at the board meeting.
- Nvidia established a National Security and Export Control Committee, began searching for a Chief Compliance Officer, and said its independent investigation would continue.
- Huang is scheduled for a transcribed House committee interview on December 1. Nvidia's general counsel, Tim Teter, is scheduled for December 2. Neither interview has occurred as of November 23.

**Important:** Reinstatement does not conclude the investigation or resolve the outstanding congressional inquiries.

## Final verdict

I would greenlight the concept, but not the current version for immediate production. The central conceit is unusually good: a company sings a breakup anthem using its own official language, celebrates its independence with a record quarter, and then sheepishly takes its founder back while the investigation is still open. The jacket, voting hands, ringing phones, October 25 calendar and compliance-officer chair are all strong visual ideas. The biggest editorial weakness is that the narrator's romantic self-empowerment makes Nvidia look more sympathetic than its board's conduct deserves. The biggest factual weakness is the $600 billion figure without its intraday qualifier. The biggest unexpected visual issue is that the diner is recognizably connected to a real Denny's location and plaque. Fix those, make the government intervention more transparent, and let the final chorus expose the board's contradiction rather than celebrate the reunion. The joke should leave the audience laughing at corporate governance, not rooting for the company to get its boyfriend back.
