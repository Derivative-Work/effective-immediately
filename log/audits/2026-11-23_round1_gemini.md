### Audit Findings (Most Serious First)

#### 1. Fact Card: Wrong Date and Incomplete Name for the Board Committee

* **Severity:** **Must-fix**
* **Where:** Fact Card, bullet 5 ("On Nov 23, the board unanimously reinstated him... It created a National Security and Export Control Committee...")
* **What's wrong:** Nvidia did not create this committee on November 23. The board established the **National Security and Export Control Oversight Committee** on **November 17, 2026**—the same day as the Q3 earnings release. You also dropped the word "Oversight" from its official name.
* **Proposed fix:** Move the committee creation to the November 17 bullet and use the full name: *"The same day, the board established a National Security and Export Control Oversight Committee, said it did not intend to reinstate him..."*

#### 2. Likeness & IP: Stacking "Flowers" Musical and Visual Cues Too Closely

* **Severity:** **Must-fix**
* **Where:** Overall concept (genre + theme + 35mm anamorphic, hard sun, warm gold grade, solo performer dancing alone)
* **What's wrong:** Borrowing a genre is fine. Borrowing a cinematography style is fine. Stacking the *exact* musical genre (bass-forward boogie-funk disco), the *exact* lyrical trope (solo post-breakup empowerment), and the *exact* visual grammar of Miley Cyrus's "Flowers" (you even cite *The ASC*'s "Framing Up for Flowers" in your sources) creates unnecessary substantial-similarity risk—especially because Suno is generating the audio and can accidentally drift close to a reference track's groove.
* **Proposed fix:** Break at least one leg of that stool. Keep the disco-funk groove and breakup lyrics, but shift the visual language away from warm golden-hour California sunlight—for example, cold fluorescent data-center lighting or stark monochrome with one warm accent. That also fits a chip company better than a sun-drenched Hollywood Hills vibe.

#### 3. Accuracy & Politics: "Unnamed Officials" Is Already Outdated as of Today

* **Severity:** **Should-fix**
* **Where:** Bridge ("Senior levels, oh, senior levels"), "SENIOR LEVELS" caller ID tag ("the release doesn't name the officials"), and Fact Card bullet 5.
* **What's wrong:** While it is true that Nvidia's November 23 press release did not name the officials, **President Trump publicly claimed credit today**, telling reporters "I believe my intervention was very important," "The board decided to do what we wanted them to do," and that his administration assured Nvidia there were no national security concerns. Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent had also publicly walked back ruling out Huang's return. Leaving the video framed around mysterious "unnamed officials" makes it look out of date on the day it's written.
* **Proposed fix:** If you still want to exclude named government officials on purpose, keep the "SENIOR LEVELS" gag in the song, but update the Fact Card to say: *"citing input from senior U.S. government officials (unnamed in the release, though the White House publicly took credit the same day)."*

#### 4. Softness Toward Nvidia: The Earnings "Glow-Up" Risks Playing Straight

* **Severity:** **Should-fix**
* **Where:** Verse 2 (`A hundred fifteen billion, up a hundred and three / Racks rolling out and I'm doing so well... (Don't ask when the quarter closed)`) and Post-Chorus (`We've got this, we've got this`).
* **What's wrong:** The irony of Verse 2 hinges entirely on the parenthetical `(Don't ask when the quarter closed)`. In AI-generated music (Suno), parenthetical ad-libs frequently get buried in the mix, mumbled, or dropped. If the listener misses that line—and doesn't catch the quick shot of the face-down October 25 desk calendar—Verse 2 reads like an unironic hype track for Nvidia's $115.8bn quarter.
* **Proposed fix:** Promote the timing critique out of a parenthetical whisper and into a main lyric line, and hold the October 25 calendar shot long enough to read clearly. Example: *"A hundred fifteen billion, up a hundred and three / All booked in October, before you left me."*

#### 5. Fairness to Anthropic & Claude: The Green Robot Probe Muddies the Facts

* **Severity:** **Should-fix**
* **Where:** Video Plan ("A small green robot 'probe' standing for Claude is one of the hands voting him back in.")
* **What's wrong:** Including Claude's proxy as a voting hand in the boardroom is a clever self-indictment, but it's factually misleading—Anthropic is a customer, not a board member voting on CEO reinstatement. Worse, a viewer who hasn't reached the end disclosure card yet will have no idea what a random green robot hand in a boardroom means.
* **Proposed fix:** Keep the green robot probe out of the board's voting ring. Instead, put it in Verse 2 among the server racks holding a purchase order, or standing outside the boardroom window holding up a "NEED CHIPS" sign. That accurately represents the commercial dependency without confusing who actually voted.

#### 6. Does the Joke Work?: The Outro Diner Reference Won't Land for General Audiences

* **Severity:** **Should-fix**
* **Where:** Outro (`There's a plaque on a booth in a diner in East San Jose / Says this is where it all got started`) and Fact Card.
* **What's wrong:** To tech insiders, the East San Jose Denny's booth is iconic. To someone who knows nothing about the story, the outro suddenly pivots to a random diner, and the Fact Card only says *"The diner is generic and not affiliated with any restaurant"* without explaining why a diner is in the song at all.
* **Proposed fix:** Add one short line to the Fact Card: *"Nvidia was founded in 1993 at a diner booth in East San Jose; the diner shown is generic and not affiliated with any restaurant."*

#### 7. Weak Lyric Line: Pre-Chorus Filler

* **Severity:** **Suggestion**
* **Where:** Pre-Chorus, line 2 (`Every friend from every stage`)
* **What's wrong:** Vague filler. Compared to the sharp specificity of `Three committees calling, FBI on the line` and `Thirty-three years in a form eight-K`, this line doesn't mean anything concrete.
* **Proposed fix:** Replace it with something that sharpens the board's sudden unanimity or the stock drop—e.g., *"Every headline, every page"* or *"Every analyst on the page."*
